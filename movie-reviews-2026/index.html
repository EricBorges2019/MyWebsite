Return to Silent Hill: Very bad, but it will never overshadow the real Silent Hill 2. Still has some fucking stupid changes. The only reason I had any leniency was because I was entirely convinced that they would entirely absolve James of the thing. But yeah, let this one be forgotten.

Iron Lung: Okay for a first-time film by Markiplier. Not amazing (and the first half did drag a lot for me), but the realization that it's about the cruelty of solitary confinement made me like it a lot more, and there are some really good moments. Some banger tracks by Andrew Hulshult.

Project Hail Mary: "Delicious hope-core" is very correct. The fact there's an alien is actually a much smaller spoiler than I thought.

Hoppers: Did Not Finish. Am I expecting too much from Pixar? Am I just too old now? Have I been too spoiled by blowing so much money on Ghibli Fest? Opening with the grandma was pretty good (kinda Up-esque) and I like that the Pixar "house style" has changed away from what the AI models use, but then it starts turning into a stock kid's movie, complete with dance scene, all hope is lost, etc. I only stayed around until I was sure it was done subverting my expectations.

Mario Galaxy Movie: Had fun pointing at all the {recognizable property}, will never watch ever again. Rosalina might as well be a battery taped to a lamp. Hoping the next movie is more than another Ready Player One/Space Jam Legacy "IP exhibition" or I won't bother watching.

Cave of Forgotten Dreams 3D: Pretty interesting but oh my god the 3d conversion is so bad

Mortal Kombat 2: 5/10, some pretty cool fights. Any emotional impact really depends on watching the first movie, which I didn't.

Top Gun duology. Top Gun 1 is peak cinema (minus how it treats women, still prefer it as a film to F1). Top Gun Maverick is technically a better movie but I personally felt that it occasionally tries too hard. Scene with Iceman on the piano was good.

Speed Racer 2008. Both serious and unserious in a way that I like (also I had a bunch of the toys as a kid). Greatest climax I have ever seen, Michael Giacchino did amazing with *Reboot*.

The Mandolorian and Grogu: **OKAY/10.** Best part is when Grogu's by himself and he meets that alien fisher guy. I did believe for a bit that it was about Grogu learning to do things himself? There was a shot during that beach chase that felt entirely AI-generated even though it probably wasn't.

Finding out later that multiple characters were already pre-existing (including that bounty hunter with the dog and the hat) did retroactively hurt my opinion of the movie.

Backrooms: A bit slow, but probably one of my favorite movies I've seen this year. Final scene where it's just a montage of shots within the Backrooms, after everything that's happened, is a perfect chaser.

Masters of the Universe. Could not stand it, sorry. Intro action scene is great and the closest we may ever get to a Destiny movie, but then it leans so hard into cringe comedy that I had to put airpods in and cover my eyes. At least the action's usually pretty good, especially the bit past the halfway point where He-Man goes sicko mode on a bunch of Skeletor's minions. Daniel Pemberton composes music well, as should be expected.

Disclosure Day: An odd duck. Haven't watched a Spielberg movie in a bit but this was an interesting watch. It's a UFO film that's more about the people than the UFO's. Thought there would be a lot more cringing but there really isn't? Boyfriend character kinda sucks though. Kinda deals with religion but I didn't really peg it as the theme?

Reminds me of Megalopolis in a good way, and at least it's an actual movie. Not sure I'd watch it again but I'm glad I saw it. In the first half I noticed there was a lot frame-in-a-frame which made me go insane (LOVED IT). Got too absorbed into a move so I stopped tracking that.

Evangelion Death (True^2): An interesting recap of the Evangelion series but I'm pretty sure somewhere between its creation and the screening I saw, someone dumped the whole film into a non-linear editor and turned on the Stabilize feature, because every cut makes the frame shake a bit.

The End of Evangelion (my third viewing in five years): One of the films I've thought about the most in the 2020's (first watch in 2021?). Genuine art.

Moana Live Action: The Search for More Money

The Odyssey (C. Nolan): Not sure I got it but I did like it. I hate hearing the discourse around it...

Spider-Man: Brand New Day. Really liked it, though my only real gripes need a spoiler warning. Also I'm probably going crazy because I thought a couple lines of dialog were AI.

Only Yesterday: Missed multiple minutes, arrived when they brought that unripe pineapple home. Really liked it. Un-Hollywood.
